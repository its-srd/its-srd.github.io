Caltec jet pump

CFD validation of multiphase surface jet pump

CFD Consultant (Contract) for Caltec Production Solutions Ltd, which supplies surface jet pump systems to the oil and gas industry. June to July 2026.

Objective

A surface jet pump uses a high pressure flow to entrain a low pressure one. The objective was to establish whether a CFD model could predict that entrainment reliably enough to support the client's targets.

High pressure water Low pressure gas Nozzle Mixing tube Diffuser Mixed flow out

A surface jet pump has no moving parts. This generic sketch illustrates the operating principle and does not represent the client's geometry.

High pressure water enters from the left. The nozzle accelerates the stream into a high velocity jet, reducing its static pressure.

The resulting low pressure entrains gas through the upper inlet, around the nozzle and into the throat.

Along the mixing tube, the high velocity water carries the gas with it, and the gas disperses through the stream until the two phases are well mixed.

The diverging diffuser decelerates the mixture and recovers static pressure before discharge.

Methodology

  • Delivered the study independently, developing a multiphase VOF model in ANSYS Fluent to the client's requirements.
  • Identified a 60% underprediction of low pressure entrainment, diagnosed the cause using transient CFD and corrected the set-up.
  • Performed a parametric study across seven operating conditions and recommended the boundary conditions.
  • Issued a technical report documenting the approach, inputs, assumptions and findings.

Results

The low pressure entrainment error was reduced from 60% to within 2.5%. The recommended boundary conditions met the client's targets within 6%, and the study was delivered within one month.

Verification and validation

I validated the model against industrial reference data, matching the high pressure mass flow to within 3.5%. Any disagreement with those data was investigated and resolved rather than simply reported.
